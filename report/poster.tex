\begin{hilite}
{\fontsize{46}{55}\selectfont\bfseries\color{su} The question}\\[4mm]
Deep competing-risks models are selected and reported on \textbf{ranking}.
Lenders act on the \textbf{probability}. Does the advantage survive when the
probabilities are measured too?
\end{hilite}

\begin{panel}{Why it matters}
A mortgage ends by default, prepayment or maturity --- once one happens the
others cannot. Provisioning needs the chance of default \emph{by each date}, not
an ordering of borrowers.

\vspace{4mm}
A model can rank borrowers perfectly and still attach the wrong numbers to them.
Reserves are computed from the numbers.
\end{panel}

\begin{panel}{Data}
Freddie Mac Single-Family Loan-Level Dataset, 50{,}000-loan samples per vintage.

\end{panel}

\begin{panel}{Protocol}
One interface: every model returns a cumulative incidence per cause, so none can
be given a metric the others cannot.

\vspace{4mm}
\textbf{Models} \ Aalen--Johansen (null), cause-specific Cox, Fine--Gray, random
survival forest, DeepHit

\vspace{2mm}
\textbf{Ranking} \ IPCW time-dependent AUC

\vspace{2mm}
\textbf{Calibration} \ integrated calibration index, integrated Brier

\vspace{2mm}
\textbf{Cost} \ fit and predict seconds

\vspace{2mm}
\textbf{Splits} \ vintage-ordered, not random

\vspace{2mm}
\textbf{Budget} \ 20 DeepHit configs, 8 forest, fixed in advance, validated
out-of-time

\vspace{2mm}
\textbf{Inference} \ paired bootstrap, $B{=}500$, two-sided, $\alpha$ fixed at
0.05, Bonferroni across three metric families
\end{panel}

% ================= COLUMN 2 =================
\begin{panel}{1. The advantage reverses with regime}
AUC at 60 months, default:

\vspace{4mm}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrr@{}}
\toprule
& Crisis & Post-crisis \\
\midrule
DeepHit (tuned) & \textbf{0.8538} & 0.7280 \\
Cause-specific Cox & 0.8346 & \textbf{0.7372} \\
\midrule
Difference & $+0.0192$ & $-0.0091$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\vspace{5mm}
Both significant. But the Cox model's own training variability on crisis data is
$\pm0.0159$, so the win is barely one standard deviation of the baseline's noise
--- the \emph{classical} models are the unstable ones here.

\vspace{4mm}
The calibration penalty does \textbf{not} reverse: $+0.0040$ worse than Cox in
both cohorts.
\end{panel}

\begin{panel}{2. No gain over a random forest}
\begin{center}
\bignum{$p=0.97$ \ \ $p=0.79$}{DeepHit vs tuned random survival forest, AUC,\\crisis and post-crisis}
\end{center}

\vspace{3mm}
Both differences fall inside DeepHit's own seed variation. On crisis
\textbf{prepayment} the forest is clearly better: AUC 0.685 vs 0.620, calibration
index 0.154 vs 0.236.
\end{panel}

\begin{panel}{3. The ranking loss is harmful}
DeepHit's loss is $\alpha\cdot$likelihood $+\ (1-\alpha)\cdot$ranking. Pooled over
40 configurations:

\vspace{4mm}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrr@{}}
\toprule
$\alpha$ & Val.\ AUC & Val.\ IBS \\
\midrule
0.10 & 0.658 & 0.0334 \\
0.20 \small(library default) & 0.749 & 0.0286 \\
0.50 & 0.776 & 0.0269 \\
1.00 \small(no ranking term) & \textbf{0.776} & 0.0271 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\vspace{5mm}
More weight on ranking makes ranking \emph{and} calibration worse, monotonically.
The reference implementation's default sits inside the harmful range.
\end{panel}

% ================= COLUMN 3 =================
\begin{panel}{4. Recalibration cannot fix it}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrr@{}}
\toprule
Calibration index & Crisis & Post-crisis \\
\midrule
Raw & 0.0209 & 0.0318 \\
Recalibrated (train) & 0.0164 & 0.0297 \\
Recalibrated (oracle) & \textbf{0.0053} & \textbf{0.0082} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\vspace{5mm}
Fitted on the training vintages --- all a lender has --- rescaling changes almost
nothing, and in 10 of 16 cells makes calibration worse. Fitted on the test
vintages, an oracle nobody can build, it cuts error by a median factor of 4.2.

\vspace{4mm}
\textbf{So the penalty is not a broken calibration map. It is the vintage shift
itself: correctable in principle, unavailable in practice.}
\end{panel}

\begin{panel}{Cost}
\begin{center}
\bignum{$\approx 2{,}000\times$}{DeepHit (59\,s) versus Fine--Gray (0.03\,s)\\per fit, 150{,}000 loans}
\end{center}
Plus 14.4 minutes of hyperparameter search the classical models never need.
\end{panel}

\begin{panel}{What we cannot claim}
One dataset family. Only five bootstrap replicates per model. Covariates observed
at origination only --- no time-varying macroeconomic variables. Our recalibration
is a marginal rescaling, not the per-cause procedures of Alberge et al.\ (2026).
With 150{,}000 test loans almost any difference is significant, so we report
effect sizes against training noise throughout.
\end{panel}
